📊 ODP Scale (Ozone Depletion Potential)

R-11 = 1.0 · measures damage to the ozone layer
ODP = Ozone Depletion Potential. The ratio of the impact of a substance on the ozone layer compared to R-11 (CFC-11), which has an ODP of 1.0.
CategoryODP RangeExamplesMeaning
CFCs0.6 – 1.0R-11 (1.0), R-12 (1.0), R-113 (0.8), R-115 (0.6)Severe ozone depletion. Banned globally.
HCFCs0.01 – 0.1R-22 (0.055), R-123 (0.02), R-124 (0.022)Moderate ozone depletion. Being phased out.
HFCs0R-134a, R-410A, R-32, R-404ANo ozone depletion. Safe for the ozone layer.
HFOs / HCFOs~0R-1234yf, R-1234ze, R-1233zdNegligible ozone depletion. Very low environmental impact.
Natural0Ammonia (R-717), CO₂ (R-744), propane (R-290)Zero ozone depletion.

📊 GWP Scale (Global Warming Potential)

CO₂ = 1 · measures heat trapping over 100 years
GWP = Global Warming Potential. The ratio of the amount of heat trapped by a greenhouse gas compared to CO₂ over a 100‑year period. CO₂ has a GWP of 1.
CategoryGWP RangeExamplesMeaning
Very Low1 – 150R-1234yf (~1), R-1234ze (~1), R-1233zd (~1), R-290 (~3), R-744 (1)Minimal global warming impact. The future of the industry.
Medium150 – 1,500R-32 (675), R-454B (466), R-455A (146), R-407C (1,774 – borderline)Moderate impact. Some are being phased down.
High1,500 – 3,000R-410A (2,088), R-134a (1,430 – borderline), R-404A (3,922 – borderline)Significant global warming impact. Being phased out or phased down.
Very High3,000+R-404A (3,922), R-507 (3,985), R-12 (10,900)Extreme global warming impact. Banned or being rapidly phased out.

🧪 HFOs / HCFOs

Hydrofluoro‑olefins · no chlorine (except HCFOs) · very low GWP · A2L or A1 current 2026
HFO = Hydrofluoro‑olefin – contains hydrogen, fluorine, carbon, with a double bond (olefin). Very low GWP, short atmospheric lifetime. Often A2L (mildly flammable).
HCFO = Hydrochlorofluoro‑olefin – same as HFO but with chlorine. R‑1233zd is an HCFO, but it's non‑flammable (A1) because of its structure.

Ammonia · CO₂ · hydrocarbons · ultra‑low GWP · industrial / specialty specialty
Natural refrigerants = substances found in nature (ammonia, CO₂, propane, isobutane). Very low GWP, zero ODP. Often toxic (B1) or highly flammable (A3). Used in industrial or specialty applications.

🔗 Family connections & evolution (the "pipeline")
CFC → HCFC → HFC → HFO / A2L Each step: lower ODP (CFC → HCFC → HFC → HFO) and lower GWP.
R-12 (CFC) → R‑134a (HFC) → R‑1234yf (HFO/A2L) automotive path
R-22 (HCFC) → R‑410A (HFC) → R‑454B / R‑32 (A2L) residential path
