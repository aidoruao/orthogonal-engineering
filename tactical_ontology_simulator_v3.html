Nash Equilibrium with Skill-Dependent Payoffs

Let κ = per-entity skill (discrimination + momentum + stress) / 3
p(κ) = P(CoD rushes | skill=κ) = (1 + κ)/3
q(κ) = P(RoN holds | skill=κ) = (1 + 2κ)/5
At κ=0 (conscript): q* = 1/3, p* = 1/3
At κ=1 (graduate): q* = 3/5, p* = 2/3
The equilibrium shifts toward aggression as skill increases.
The counter-intuitive result: higher skill makes RoN play more aggressive because the RoN player can afford to breach knowing their discrimination will prevent catastrophic errors. The CoD player, conversely, becomes less reckless because skill reduces the need for compensatory speed.

Extensive Form — Sequential CQB with Skill-Weighted Beliefs

Information sets: The defender does not know if the attacker has committed to a slide or a jump-peek. The attacker does not know if the defender is deep-holding or shallow-holding. Skill modifies the belief update rate: a high-skill defender updates P(threat|noise) faster, compressing the attacker's surprise window. This is modeled as a Bayesian game with skill-weighted precision on the likelihood functions.

Yerkes-Dodson with Skill-Shifted Apex

Performance P(σ,κ) = A·σ·exp(−σ/(τ·(1+κ/2))) where κ = stress_resistance skill. Higher skill shifts the apex rightward, allowing higher arousal before performance degradation. CoD players with κ=0.9 can operate at σ=0.8 without degradation; conscripts degrade at σ=0.5.

Decision Fatigue & Cognitive Load — Skill Inoculation

CL(t,κ) = Σ_i (w_i · d_i) · (1 + α·t) · (1 − κ/2)

The CoD player with high skill doesn't just rush—they have automated the discrimination step that normally requires slow deliberation.

Pareto Frontier — Survival vs Clear Time

Each point is a Monte Carlo run at a specific (aggression, discrimination) pair. The Pareto frontier connects points where you cannot improve survival without increasing clear time, nor reduce clear time without reducing survival. The hybrid operator (high aggression + high discrimination) should lie on or near this frontier, dominating both pure CoD (fast but low survival) and pure RoN (slow but high survival).

Skill-Style Heatmap

The diagonal from (0,0) to (1,1) represents the "hybrid hypothesis"—that adding skill to aggression makes it viable. The upper-left quadrant (high aggression, low skill) is the "death zone." The lower-right quadrant (low aggression, high skill) is the "RoN safe zone."

Crossover Analysis — Skill Threshold Where CoD Overtakes RoN

X-axis: Per-entity discrimination skill κ (0 to 1). Y-axis: Win rate. The crossover point κ* is where the CoD curve intersects the RoN curve. If κ* < 1, there exists a skill level at which speed with discrimination beats pure caution. If κ* does not exist within [0,1], pure RoN dominates at all skill levels under current geometry.

The gap at κ=0 is the "structural advantage" of RoN—geometry minus skill. The gap at κ=1 is the "skill advantage" of CoD—speed plus discrimination. If the gap changes sign between κ=0 and κ=1, the crossover exists and is calculable by binary search on the skill axis.

Crossover Model Specification

W_CoD(κ) = W_CoD(0) + (W_CoD(1) − W_CoD(0)) · κ^δ [δ = 1.5, diminishing returns]
W_RoN(κ) = W_RoN(0) + (W_RoN(1) − W_RoN(0)) · κ^ε [ε = 0.8, linear returns]
κ* solves: W_CoD(κ*) = W_RoN(κ*)
If no solution in [0,1]: RoN strictly dominates ∀κ
The diminishing returns exponent δ > 1 for CoD reflects that geometry is a hard constraint—skill helps, but cannot fully overcome the fatal funnel. The linear returns ε < 1 for RoN reflects that skill compounds caution: a careful operator who is also skilled becomes nearly unbeatable. The crossover is therefore not guaranteed—it depends on the room complexity and physics fidelity parameters.
Falsifiability Audit — Crossover: falsifies_if: κ* < 0 or κ* > 1 (outside valid skill range); falsifies_if: W_CoD(κ) or W_RoN(κ) > 1 or < 0; falsifies_if: crossover computed without monotonicity check (W_CoD and W_RoN must both be monotonic in κ); falsifies_if: δ or ε ≤ 0 (impossible returns exponent).
