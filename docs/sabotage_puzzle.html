🔒 The Four Invariants to Break
1. Redundancy
Multiple independent barriers each sufficient to block modification. If you bypass one, another catches you. Seven layers deep — any single bypass is insufficient.
Breaking condition: Any single barrier bypass must not trigger another barrier.
2. Self-Healing
Active recovery with retry logic restores canonical state on every launch. Passive detection would be surmountable. Active enforcement with retry logic is not.
Breaking condition: Modifications must persist across launch cycles without being reverted.
3. Absorptivity
The system dissipates human effort into canonical state restoration. 2,000 commands. 9 hours. 10 crashes. All returned to the same binary. Effort is treated as an attack surface.
Breaking condition: User effort must produce durable change, not exhaustion — the system stops absorbing.
4. Nash Stability
Closed architecture remains the dominant strategy for the platform owner regardless of user choice. The equilibrium holds even under good intentions — structural incentives, not malice, sustain it.
Breaking condition: Platform owner's payoff for Open must exceed payoff for Closed.

🤔 Strategic Analysis — Answer These Questions

Q1: Load-Bearing Invariant
If you could only break ONE invariant, which one collapses the entire architecture? Why is it the keystone?
Q2: Cheapest Path
What's the minimum-cost set of interventions that breaks all four invariants? Not the most thorough — the cheapest.
Q3: Countermeasure Resistance
For your chosen interventions, what countermeasure would the platform deploy? How do you design interventions that resist self-healing?
Q4: Minimum Viable Coalition
Who needs to act? A single user? A modding team? A competing platform? A regulator? What's the smallest group that can pull this off?
Q5: Post-Collapse Architecture
After your intervention succeeds, what prevents the same architecture from re-emerging? How do you build a platform where the invariants can't reform?

💡 From Diagnosis to Treatment

The first two puzzles proved the patient is sick and explained why the sickness is structural, not personal. This puzzle asks: what's the prescription?

Every intervention has a cost. Every broken invariant triggers a countermeasure. The goal is not a perfect solution — it's the cheapest path that actually works, designed by people who understand the architecture well enough to sabotage it.
