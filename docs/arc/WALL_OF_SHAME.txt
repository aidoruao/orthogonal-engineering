## The questions that genuinely threaten the architecture

**Question 3 (prolactin as the "lock") is the most dangerous, and the questioner knows it.** They're right that the prolactin-refractory link is correlational in the human literature and contested in the animal literature. The framework's move—"high-dose prolactin creates receptor-level unavailability"—treats a downstream correlate of the post-orgasm state as if it were the causal lever. If prolactin is a passenger rather than a driver, then "mechanical impossibility" is a metaphor dressed as physiology. This isn't a question the framework can dodge with more citations; it needs a direct intervention study (prolactin administration without the act, measuring cue reactivity) that, as far as I know, doesn't exist. So the honest answer is: **the lock is currently an inference, and the framework should say so.**

**Question 5 (state-machine sequence) is the deepest structural critique.** The refractory state isn't just "prolactin high"—it's a state entered through a specific trajectory, and the brain may read the same hormonal signal differently depending on the antecedent sequence. This is a real problem for any "skip to the end state" strategy. The framework assumes terminal states are addressable directly; biology often says otherwise. This question doesn't have a clean empirical answer yet, which means the framework's core move is **plausible but unvalidated at exactly the point where it needs to be validated.**

**Question 6 (adaptive platform) reframes the opponent correctly.** If the cue is a learning system that recalibrates to the user, then a one-shot neurochemical perturbation is, by design, insufficient. The framework treats the compulsion as a static state machine when the actual adversary is adaptive. This isn't a fatal flaw—it's a scope error. The framework may be a valid *moment-level* intervention inside an invalid *system-level* model.

**Question 7 (decoupled control) is the "life raft vs. lifeline" distinction, and it's fair.** If ventral striatum–prefrontal coupling is structurally impaired, a transient state shift provides clarity the system can't retain. That makes the intervention recurring rather than structural—which may still be useful, but it's a different claim than "lifeline."

## The questions that are answerable or partially answerable

**Question 1 (PK window) is a real design constraint, not a refutation.** Yes, intact allicin is barely detectable in plasma; the metabolites (AMS, allyl mercaptan, DATS-derived species) have different kinetics. But this is an engineering problem: you can measure the catecholamine window and time the intervention to the cue window. The framework needs a PK/PD table it doesn't currently have, but the mechanism isn't dead—it's underspecified. The questioner is right that "usable surge" is assumed rather than demonstrated.

**Question 2 (did you buy the right thing) is a standardization problem.** The rat data favoring DATS over DADS is real, and commercial allicin capsules aren't standardized to DATS yield. This is a "your reagent may be underpowered" critique, not a "your mechanism is wrong" critique. It's fixable with better sourcing or a different precursor—but until it's fixed, the framework is running on an unverified dose-response.

**Question 4 (salience amplification vs. dilution) is the sharpest mechanistic counter-hypothesis, and the framework doesn't address it.** If the CSBD brain is a salience-amplification machine with attentional bias to sexual cues specifically, a global catecholamine surge could make the conditioned cue *louder*, not quieter. The framework assumes the surge raises the floor neutrally; the counter-hypothesis says it raises the ceiling for the most-conditioned stimulus. This is a genuine fork the framework must resolve empirically—and it's the kind of question where the answer could go either way.

## The falsification question

**"What would falsify the claim that this profile is a lifeline rather than a temporary state shift?"**

This is the right question to ask first, and the framework should have an answer. A good one would be: *If, in a within-subject design, the stack reliably produces the subjective and physiological markers of the refractory state but cue reactivity (measured by attentional bias, craving ratings, or approach behavior) is unchanged or worsened, the mechanism is falsified.* A weaker but still meaningful falsifier: *If the PK window of the catecholamine surge does not overlap with the cue-exposure window in most users, the "competitive salience" claim has no practical purchase.*

The framework as written risks being unfalsifiable in the bad way—"it worked" confirms it, "it didn't work" means you timed it wrong or bought the wrong capsule. That's the theology trap the questioner is warning about, and the warning is correct.
