📌 What you’ll actually handle (2026)
Residential AC / heat pumps: R‑32, R‑454B (A2L). You’ll also service existing R‑22 and R‑410A.
Light commercial: R‑32, R‑454B, R‑1234ze (chillers).
Automotive / MVAC: R‑1234yf (A2L) — also R‑134a in older cars.
Chillers / large commercial: R‑1233zd (A1, non‑flammable) and R‑1234ze (A2L).
⛔ What you will not deal with (or rarely)
CFCs (R-12, R-11): Banned. Ozone depleters. You might see them in museum‑old systems, but not in your daily work.
R‑717 (ammonia) / R‑290 / R‑600a: Industrial, toxic, or highly flammable. Unless you go into supermarket or industrial refrigeration, you won’t touch these.
R‑404A / R‑507: High‑GWP supermarket refrigerants. Being phased out fast. You may encounter them if you do commercial freezer work, but they’re being replaced.
📖 How to read this table
• ASHRAE class: A1 = non‑toxic, non‑flammable · A2L = mildly flammable · A3 = highly flammable · B1 = toxic, non‑flammable.
• GWP (Global Warming Potential): CO₂ = 1. The lower the number, the better for the environment.
• ODP (Ozone Depletion Potential): 0 = safe for the ozone layer. Anything above 0 damages it. CFCs are 1.0, which is why they were banned.
• Status 2026: “✔ current” = new systems use this · “phase‑out” = still around but being replaced · “retired” = no new systems, you’ll only service it · “industrial/specialty” = not residential.
• Cylinder colors: Always read the label. Colors are a visual guide, not a legal standard.
