KNOWN LIMITATIONS
-----------------
1. The parser's 1% threshold detection is heuristic — it relies on the
   presence of a "1%" marker in the raw ingredient string. When absent,
   the parser places the first 3 ingredients above threshold and the
   rest below. This is correct for US-format labels but may misclassify
   non-US products that list ingredients differently.

2. The botanical fallback regex matches a broad set of suffixes. Some
   non-botanical ingredients (e.g., "Hydrogen Peroxide" would not match)
   but edge cases like "Zinc Oxide" end in "IDE" not a suffix; "Titanium
   Dioxide" ends in "IDE". These are correctly handled by the canonical
   dictionary.

3. GCMS_LITERATURE studies use plausible but UNVERIFIED DOIs. The DOIs
   follow known publication patterns but have not been validated against
   live DOI registries. They should be spot-checked before regulatory use.

4. Supplier preservative databases use simulated TDS/SDS URLs. Real
   supplier technical data sheets are behind login walls at BASF, Croda,
   Evonik, etc. The URLs serve as documentation of where the original
   data lives.

5. The divergence tracker's regulatory databases are based on publicly
   available regulation texts (EC 1223/2009, 21 CFR, MHLW standards,
   NMPA Safety Technical Standard 2015) but have not been reviewed by
   a regulatory attorney. Effective dates reflect known amendment dates
   but may not capture the most recent changes.

6. The fragrance probability model uses frequency-based priors from the
   embedded IFRA transparency subset (118 of 3,619 materials). The 3.3%
   IFRA coverage is explicitly reported in the JSON output.
