## MODULE 1: REVERSE-ENGINEERING "THE 1% RULE" LOOPHOLE

### The Regulatory Mechanism
Under INCI standards (EU Regulation 1223/2009, FDA 21 CFR 701.3), ingredients above 1% must be listed in descending order by weight. Below 1%, order is arbitrary. This creates two exploitable asymmetries:

### Tactic 1A: Marketing Dusting
- Front-of-bottle claims: "With Argan Oil!" "Infused with Aloe Vera!"
- Reality: Botanical extract at 0.01%–0.1%, placed below the 1% line in random order
- Detection method: Patent data shows actual formulation percentages. Example: US Patent 9,439,857 (L'Oreal, 2016) lists argan oil at 0.05% in a shampoo where SLES is 12%.
- Visual deconstruction needed: Side-by-side comparison of marketing claim vs. patent-derived concentration

### Tactic 1B: Chemical Masking
- Harsh preservatives (DMDM hydantoin, formaldehyde-releasers) at 0.5%–0.9%
- Milder ingredients (glycerin, panthenol) also at 0.5%–0.9% placed ABOVE the harsh ones
- Consumer reads label top-to-bottom: "Glycerin, Panthenol, DMDM Hydantoin..." — creates false impression of gentleness
- The 1% line is the inflection point. Everything above it is ordered by concentration. Everything below it is ordered by corporate choice.

### v3.0 Implementation Requirements
1. Build a visual "1% Line Detector" — an interactive SVG or CSS visualization that shows:
   - A sample ingredient list with the 1% threshold marked
   - Color coding: above 1% = ordered by weight (green zone), below 1% = arbitrary order (red zone)
   - Hover/click to reveal patent-derived actual concentrations for common products

2. Include a "Dusting Calculator" — simple JavaScript that:

   - Outputs: "Claimed: Argan Oil. Patent-derived concentration: 0.03%. Marketing dusting confirmed."

3. Document the exact legal text:

## MODULE 2: EXPOSING UPSTREAM RAW MATERIAL PRESERVATIVES

### The Supply Chain Mechanism
- Bulk raw materials (surfactant blends, botanical extracts, oils) are preserved by the SUPPLIER, not the BRAND
- Example: A "paraben-free" shampoo uses a surfactant blend purchased from BASF or Croda that contains methylparaben at 0.3% as a raw material preservative
- The brand does not add parabens during final manufacturing → legally claims "Paraben-Free"
- The parabens are present in the final product via CARRYOVER from the raw material
- Current labeling gap: No requirement to disclose upstream preservatives if they are "incidental" or below 0.01% in final formulation

3. Document the regulatory loophole:
