Abstract

This document presents orthogonal engineering: a constraint-first design methodology for extracting reliable functionality from systems that structurally cannot meet their stated guarantees. Rather than attempting to correct or suppress constraint-induced failure modes, the approach routes them into structural canals that preserve invariants.

Core Definitions

Benevolent Absence
A system condition in which promised functionality F cannot be delivered in required form S, yet produces an extractable approximation F′ embedded within structural drift D.
Invariant
A property of system output that remains stable under constraint-induced drift and can be structurally extracted without semantic correction.
Canal Architecture
A structural pathway that diverts entropy, drift, or verbosity away from the analysis layer while allowing invariant signal to pass through.

Benevolent Absence Theorem

Systems exhibiting benevolent absence can be made reliable through structural extraction rather than constraint correction.

Evidence: LLM outputs consistently embed task-relevant answers within verbosity and drift, enabling invariant extraction without modifying model behavior.

Methodology

Orthogonal engineering proceeds by identifying the constrained axis of a system and designing perpendicular structures that treat failure modes as expected flow. Analysis operates on timing, structure, and sequence rather than semantic interpretation.

Drift flows into the canal, leaving the signal clean.
