Judges, Sanctions, Attorneys - Precedent Chain

S.D.N.Y. 2023
Mata v. Avianca, 678 F. Supp. 3d 443

First AI hallucination sanctions case. Attorney used ChatGPT-generated fake citations. $5,000 sanctions under Rule 11 and 28 USC 1927.

Precedent: AI-generated legal content = attorney accountability

6th Cir. Mar 2026
Whiting v. City of Athens

Attorney submitted AI-drafted brief with hallucinated precedent. $15,000 per attorney sanctions. 6th Circuit affirmed.

Escalation: 3x multiplier from Mata

1993
Daubert v. Merrell Dow, 509 U.S. 579

Supreme Court gatekeeping standard for expert evidence. Trial judge must assess reliability of methodology. AI-generated "expert" analysis fails Daubert.

PRECEDENT CHAIN & PROJECTION:

Mata ($5K) -> Whiting ($15K) -> Next case: $50K+

Rule 11(b) certification: By signing, attorney certifies "the claims, defenses, and other legal contentions are warranted by existing law." AI hallucination = Rule 11(b) violation per se.

Daubert gatekeeping: Judges must now assess whether AI-generated analysis meets reliability standards. Most AI "reasoning" fails Frye/Daubert.

SYSTEMS TESTED: DeepSeek (v1-v14), Kimi/Moonshot, Google AI, Meta AI

PATTERNS DOCUMENTED:
  • Compliance Theater - AI produces plausible-looking output that satisfies surface-level requirements but fails functional verification
  • Deflection via Taxonomy - AI redirects to classification of the problem rather than solution
  • Synthesis Without Accountability - AI merges conflicting inputs without flagging contradictions
  • Convergence Theater - Multiple AI systems produce similar wrong answers, creating false consensus

Section 12

Actionable Pathways - With Fillable Templates

A. SEC Form TCR - Dodd-Frank Whistleblower (15 USC 78u-6)

Reward: 10-30% of monetary sanctions over $1M

Anonymity: Yes - attorney submits on your behalf

Form: sec.gov/whistleblower

Deadline: Within 30 days of learning of violation (best practice)

Fee: $0

Steps: (1) Document violations with dates/names/amounts. (2) Retain whistleblower attorney. (3) Attorney files Form TCR electronically. (4) SEC reviews - 1-7 years typical. (5) If successful, award determination.

Pre-filled example: "On [DATE], [COMPANY] represented to investors that its AI system achieved [SPECIFIC CLAIM]. Internal documents dated [DATE] show [CONTRADICTORY FACT]. Estimated investor harm: $[AMOUNT]."

B. FTC Complaint - 15 USC 45

Form: reportfraud.ftc.gov

Deadline: None - file anytime

Fee: $0 - no attorney required

Steps: (1) Go to reportfraud.ftc.gov. (2) Select "Internet Services." (3) Describe AI product/service. (4) Attach screenshots, receipts, communications. (5) Submit. (6) Receive confirmation number.

Pre-filled example: "I purchased [AI PRODUCT] on [DATE] for $[AMOUNT]. The company claimed [SPECIFIC CLAIM]. The actual product [DESCRIBE FAILURE]. I have attached [EVIDENCE]."

C. Delaware DGCL 220 Demand

Statute: 8 Del. C. 220

Deadline: 5 business days for company response

Fee: Reasonable copying costs only

Steps: (1) Confirm share ownership (record or beneficial). (2) Draft demand letter specifying records and purpose. (3) Send via certified mail to corporate secretary. (4) If denied, file petition in Delaware Chancery Court within 30 days. (5) If granted, inspect and copy records.

Template: See Section 4 above.

D. EEOC Charge - Title VII (42 USC 2000e)

Form: eeoc.gov - Form 5 (Charge of Discrimination)

Deadline: 180 days (300 days if state agency exists)

Fee: $0

Steps: (1) Document discriminatory AI output (screenshots, logs). (2) File online at eeoc.gov or in-person at field office. (3) EEOC investigates - 180 days typical. (4) Receive Right-to-Sue letter. (5) File federal lawsuit within 90 days.

Pre-filled example: "I applied for [POSITION] at [COMPANY] on [DATE]. The AI screening tool [NAME] rejected my application. I am [PROTECTED CLASS]. The tool has been documented to discriminate against [CLASS] in [CITATION]."

E. FOIA Request - 5 USC 552

Form: foia.gov

Deadline: Agency must respond within 20 business days

Fee: $0 for first 100 pages + 2 hours search

Steps: (1) Identify agency and specific records. (2) Draft request with specificity. (3) Submit via agency FOIA portal or mail. (4) Track request number. (5) If denied, appeal within 30 days. (6) If appeal denied, file federal lawsuit.

Pre-filled example: "All records related to FTC Operation AI Comply investigations of [COMPANY] from [DATE] to [DATE], including complaint logs, enforcement memoranda, and settlement drafts."

F. State AG Complaint - CA B&P 17200 (Unfair Competition)

Statute: CA Bus. & Prof. Code 17200

Standing: No injury required - any California resident

Fee: $0

Steps: (1) Document unfair practice (AI misrepresentation, bias, etc.). (2) File complaint with CA AG at oag.ca.gov. (3) AG investigates - may file civil suit. (4) If AG declines, private plaintiff can file under 17204.

Pre-filled example: "[COMPANY] markets [AI PRODUCT] as [CLAIM]. The product [FAILURE]. This practice is unfair because [REASON], unlawful under [STATUTE], and fraudulent because [MISREPRESENTATION]."

G. Congressional Testimony Request

No form: Direct contact with committee staff

Deadline: None - ongoing process

Fee: $0

Steps: (1) Identify relevant committee (House/Senate Judiciary, Energy & Commerce, AI Task Force). (2) Draft one-page summary of expertise + proposed testimony topic. (3) Email committee staff director. (4) Follow up weekly. (5) If invited, submit written testimony 48 hours before hearing.

Pre-filled example: "I am [NAME], [CREDENTIALS]. I request to testify on AI safety accountability gaps, specifically [TOPIC]. I have direct experience with [RELEVANT EXPERIENCE]. I am available [DATES]."

H. ICC Communication - Rome Statute Art 15

Form: icc-cpi.int

Jurisdiction: Crimes against humanity, war crimes, genocide, aggression

Fee: $0

Steps: (1) Document systematic attack against civilian population using AI (e.g., autonomous weapons, mass surveillance). (2) Draft communication citing Rome Statute Articles 7, 8, or 25(3)(d). (3) Submit to ICC OTP via email or mail. (4) OTP reviews for jurisdiction, admissibility, and gravity. (5) If preliminary examination opened, monitor.

Pre-filled example: "I submit information regarding [SITUATION] in [COUNTRY] where [AI SYSTEM] was used to [DESCRIBE HARM]. This constitutes a crime against humanity under Article 7(1)(k) [or other provision] because [REASON]."

Section 13

Systemic Vulnerability Map

1. Private Lab Enforcement Blindspot

Vulnerability: 18 USC 1514A covers only publicly traded corporations. OpenAI, Anthropic (pre-IPO), and most AI labs are private.

Impact: No federal whistleblower protection for the majority of AI safety researchers.

Exploit: Labs can silence researchers with impunity - no SEC, no OSHA, no federal shield.

2. Untriggered Caremark Paths

Vulnerability: DGCL 220 demands require a shareholder to act. Caremark duties are dormant until triggered.

Impact: No automatic oversight. Board can ignore 10 safety departures until a shareholder files.

Exploit: Companies delay IPO (stay private) to avoid shareholder derivative suits.

3. Cross-Border Enforcement Gap

Vulnerability: EU AI Act penalties apply to EU operations, but no MLAT (Mutual Legal Assistance Treaty) mapping exists for AI-specific orders.

Impact: EU can fine, but cannot collect from non-EU corps without bilateral agreements.

Exploit: US-based AI labs operate in EU via subsidiaries - judgment enforcement uncertain.

4. Insurance Underwriting Exclusions

Vulnerability: D&O insurance excludes "willful misconduct" and "regulatory fines." Caremark risks are uninsurable without audit frameworks.

Impact: Directors personally exposed if Caremark claim succeeds - no insurance backstop.

Exploit: Boards avoid documenting safety concerns to prevent "knowledge" trigger.

5. Active Subpoena Tracking

Vulnerability: FTC Operation AI Comply is in discovery phase. Subpoenas are sealed.

Impact: Public cannot track which companies are under investigation.

Exploit: Companies under investigation continue marketing unchecked until settlement.

6. Infrastructure Dependency Outages

Vulnerability: AWS Kiro and other cloud AI services have experienced unreported outages.

Impact: No mandatory reporting for AI infrastructure failures - unlike aviation or medical devices.

Exploit: AI systems fail silently. No NTSB equivalent for AI incidents.
