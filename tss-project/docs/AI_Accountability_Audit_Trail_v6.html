Every consumer who pays for AI services has a contractual and statutory right to the same model capability, context window, and feature set as enterprise clients. The asymmetry is not a product tier — it is a discriminatory practice that violates:

Every technique, tool, and method used by AI corporations to maximize their own AI capability is publicly documented in research papers, patent filings, and open-source repositories. The asymmetry is not technical — it is access. The clean room toolkit reconstructs enterprise-grade AI capability from public sources, eliminating the vendor lock-in that sustains the asymmetry.

To maintain legal defensibility, the clean room must be constructed from publicly available information only:

Consumer AI Parity Class Action: The Ultimate Asymmetry Inversion

THE CLASS ACTION THEORY: Every consumer who purchased AI services based on capability claims that were knowingly false because the enterprise version was superior has a claim for unfair/deceptive practices. The class is every paying consumer of every major AI service. The damages are the difference between what was promised and what was delivered, plus punitive damages for deliberate deception.

CLASS ACTION COMPLAINT TEMPLATE: Consumer AI Parity

Case Caption: [LEAD PLAINTIFF], individually and on behalf of all others similarly situated, v. [AI CORPORATION], Defendant.

Jurisdiction: Federal Court [District] — 28 USC 1332 (diversity) + 15 USC 45 (FTC Act federal question)

Class Definition: All persons who purchased [AI PRODUCT] from [DATE] to [DATE] based on defendant's representations of capability, performance, or safety that were materially false because defendant deployed a superior version for enterprise/internal use while delivering a degraded version to consumers.

Causes of Action:

  • Count I: 15 USC 45 (FTC Act) — Unfair/deceptive practices (class-wide deception)
  • Count II: 15 USC 1125(a) (Lanham Act) — False advertising (capability claims)
  • Count III: State consumer protection act — Unfair/deceptive trade practices (all 50 states)
  • Count IV: Breach of implied warranty — Fitness for purpose (degraded model unfit)
  • Count V: Unjust enrichment — Defendant retained profits from degraded product
  • Count VI: RICO 18 USC 1962(c) — Pattern of fraud across product lines (if multiple products)

Damages: (1) Restitution of all fees paid; (2) Difference in value between promised and delivered product; (3) Punitive damages (willful, wanton deception); (4) Attorney fees; (5) Injunctive relief — order defendant to deliver parity product.

Expert Testimony: Clean room engineer demonstrates identical public-source reconstruction of enterprise capability, proving degradation was deliberate, not technical.
