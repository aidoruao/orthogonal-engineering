## 1. Indexer top-k 512 — the amplitude gate is binary [hypothesis]
- **V4 subsystem:** Lightning Indexer, top-k 512 of ~250k CSA pools, ReLU-gated scores.
- **Edge case:** importance in Mem|8 is a continuous amplitude A; top-k is a hard cutoff. Token #513 in relevance order is *absent* — the "near-miss edge." ReLU zeroing of negative scores adds a second hard threshold. The index also holds ~0.2% of pools (512/250k) — recall coverage is structurally thin.
- **Resolution:** soft top-k — retain top-k but weight pooled entries by normalized score (score/Σscores) inside the indexed representation; during post-training, a Gumbel-softmax relaxation makes the cutoff differentiable, reverting to hard at inference. **Measure:** index diversity (unique source tokens in top-512) and MRCR-1M needle recall at 1M ctx vs baseline 78.7.

## 2. Index redundancy — the same token wins 4 times [hypothesis]
- **V4 subsystem:** CSA rate-4 overlapping windows → each token sits in ~4 pools → up to 4 index entries per source token.
- **Edge case (quantified):** wave interference with no damping. At 1M ctx there are 262,144 CSA pools/layer; the index holds 512. With ~4-fold token overlap and uniform scoring, the expected unique-token ratio in top-512 is ≈ **25%** (512/4) — three of every four index slots are duplicates of already-retrieved tokens. That is the frequency-bias edge, displacing diverse recall.
- **Resolution:** score-dampen by multiplicity (divide entry score by its source token's pool count) or first-occurrence dedup → restores ≈4× effective index diversity at zero storage cost. **Measure:** top-512 unique-token ratio at 1M ctx; expect 25% → ~75%+ with no architecture change.

## 3. HCA rate 128 — fixed ω, phase-blind boundaries [hypothesis]
- **V4 subsystem:** HCA compresses non-overlapping windows of 128 at fixed rate; no indexer on HCA.
- **Edge case:** Mem|8 treats recall rate ω as a *parameter*, not a constant. A critical token at window position 0 vs 127 gets different context (phase sensitivity); window-boundary information is discarded (non-overlap = no interference structure). HCA recall is all-or-nothing.
- **Resolution:** content-adaptive rate — modulate pooling rate by indexer importance (high-amplitude regions pool at 32, low at 128) keeping the same mean KV budget; or boundary-overlap (pool last 8 tokens of each HCA window into the next, phase-aware). **Measure:** KV size @1M (6 GB target unchanged) vs HCA-pool recall on long-range probes; target 2–4M ctx at the same 6 GB (V5-2) becomes the arithmetic test.

## 4. KV budget vs context — decay replaces uniform rate hikes [hypothesis]
- **V4 subsystem:** pools are retained without decay; verified KV ≈ 6 GB @1M = **21 CSA ≈ 5.7 GB (94%) + 20 HCA ≈ 0.17 GB + sliding ≈ 0** — the budget is CSA-dominated, because rate 4 is the finest (finest = biggest).
- **Edge case (arithmetic):** at 2M ctx total KV doubles to ≈ **11.7 GB** (CSA ≈ 11.4 GB, HCA ≈ 0.34 GB) unless rates rise; at 4M it quadruples to ≈ 23.4 GB. Gabor says Δt·Δf ≥ 1/4π: a uniform rate hike buys storage at the price of temporal resolution *everywhere*, including where it matters.
- **Resolution:** importance-decayed pool retention (Mem|8 D(t)) — on write, evict lowest-amplitude pools; coarsen old low-amplitude pools (merge adjacent pairs) instead of uniform re-rate. Because CSA holds 94% of the budget, decay targets CSA pools first (coarsen low-importance CSA pools 4→8 or merge, keeping HCA untouched). **Measure:** 2M ctx ≈ 6 GB fp8 with recall deltas only in bottom-decile pools; the V5-2 target (2–4M at same budget) becomes a pure arithmetic test of the decay schedule.

## 5. attn_sink — the context field E is static [hypothesis]
- **V4 subsystem:** per-head learnable attention sink.
- **Edge case:** Mem|8's E(x,t) is a *dynamic* context field; V4's sink is learned once and fixed. Agentic traces (tool outputs, error markers, diff hunks) deserve boosted salience at inference, but the sink cannot express it; deeper layers' hc_mix may also damp the sink signal.
- **Resolution:** task-conditioned sink modulation — a small adapter on the sink embedding trained on agentic traces (tool-result tokens get +Δ sink), 6 MB-class parameter cost. **Measure:** Terminal Bench 2.1 (82.7 baseline) and MRCR-1M; the FA/agentic gain is the target.

## 6. Hash-MoE bootstrap — OOV and collision edges [hypothesis → MEASURED]
- **V4 subsystem:** frozen tid2eid table on layers 0–2; vocab 129,280.
- **Edge case (two):** (a) hash collisions — two frequent tokens mapped to one expert → bootstrap-layer oversubscription; (b) tokenizer-v2 continuation tokens (V5-1) are *unmapped* — the frozen table has no defined fallback for new token ids.
- **Probe result (this session, `v4_bootstrap_load_probe.py`, REAL V4 tokenizer):** on real code (oe-local tree, 109K lines / 927K tokens / 17,627 unique ids, Zipf s ≈ 1.10) a *uniform-random* tid2eid assignment yields **max expert load ≈ 9.9× the mean** (max expert 3.8% of traffic vs 0.39% mean, min 0.09×); on the templated generator corpus (1,227 unique ids, s ≈ 1.18) skew is **≈ 21.4×**. Top-10 token ids carry ≈ 20% of all traffic. The adversarial all-collide bound is ≈ 242–256×.
- **Resolution (upgraded by the measurement):** (a) the distribution alone guarantees ~10× skew *even with a perfect hash* — and **`bootstrap_pin_probe.py` (8/5 evening, real V4 tokenizer, 4 domains) shows the pin-100 construction does NOT reduce that skew either: pin-P residual (P = 50/100/256, sha256 tail) ≈ base skew (generator 21.5× / real_clean 9.9× / benchmarks 8.2× / canonical_mathlogic 10.6×), because the max-load expert carries the #1 token's INDIVISIBLE mass (8.4% / 3.9% / 3.2% / 4.2% — constant across P)**. Pin-100's actual value = collision-freedom for the head (~20 expected naive-hash collisions among the top-100 removed); its load-balancing claim is FALSIFIED. Heads are corpus-specific (top-100 Jaccards 0.09–0.33; a foreign pin list covers 15–44% of a surface's mass and buys zero skew reduction there). Bootstrap-layer skew is therefore structural (Zipf s ≈ 1.15–1.58) and irreducible under 1-tid-1-expert routing — options: accept (frozen layers, 6/256 active), multi-slot hashing for the top-K tids, or learned routing; (b) define the extension rule *now* for tokenizer-v2 continuation tokens: map new ids by hash of their subword merge path, documented with the tokenizer experiment. **Measure (next):** if the real tid2eid table becomes available (weights), verify top-100 slots are collision-free and re-run the probe on it.

## 7. 6/256 routing — entropy collapse on low-entropy domains [hypothesis]
- **V4 subsystem:** noaux_tc top-6, Sqrt(Softplus), routed scale 1.5; 97.7% idle.
- **Edge case:** admissibility as maximum-dissipation selection — routing is a constraint-check, but with no load-balance term, repetitive domains (code: LiveCodeBench 91.6 says the code distribution is low-entropy) can collapse traffic onto a few experts, leaving the admissibility region unexercised; 97.7% idle is the un-pruned tail.
- **Resolution:** entropy-constrained routing — a small auxiliary entropy term on gate logits during post-training (differentiable, no aux loss at inference); plus per-domain expert-affinity telemetry (the census) to pin the coldest 30% for pruning and the hottest per domain. **Measure:** routing entropy per domain; census output is the prune list (158 → ≈110 GB path).

## 8. MTP/DSpark — confidence only at the last step [hypothesis]
- **V4 subsystem:** 3 MTP blocks; markov_head (rank 256) + confidence_head exist only on mtp.2; DSpark block 5, 7-token speculative window.
- **Edge case:** the markov draft is domain-entropy-blind — on low-entropy code it wins big (91.6), on HLE-style novel reasoning it is near-uniform, so the 7-token speculative window *pays draft cost for nothing*; and with confidence only at mtp.2, there is no early-exit signal for MTP-1/2.
- **Resolution:** confidence-gated decode — if mtp.2 confidence is low, skip DSpark and single-token decode (admissibility: reject the perturbation); add lightweight confidence heads to mtp.0/1 for hierarchical early stop; adaptive block size by domain entropy telemetry. **Measure:** DSpark accept-rate per domain (code vs HLE); tokens/query.

## 9. Hyper-connections — Sinkhorn ×20 is unconditional [hypothesis]
- **V4 subsystem:** hc_mix per block, Sinkhorn-Knopp doubly-stochastic mixing, 20 iterations, non-expansive claim.
- **Edge case:** 20 iterations × 43 layers is fixed cost regardless of condition; sheaf gluing says local-to-global consistency matters most at depth — shallow mixing is cheap, deep mixing is load-bearing. A saturating hc_mix coefficient is a silent gradient-damping edge in deep layers.
- **Resolution:** adaptive Sinkhorn — early-stop when doubly-stochastic error < ε (measurable in the forward pass; expect the mean iteration count to fall well below 20); per-layer hc_scale init by depth (preserve shallow, mix deep). **Measure:** forward-pass iteration count, gradient norms per depth on the probe corpus.

## 10. Test-time effort — the monotone-descent controller [hypothesis]
- **V4 subsystem:** reasoning-effort ladder (non-think → think → max); measured HLE 8.1 → 34.8 = 4.3× from test-time scaling alone.
- **Edge case:** the ladder is not query-adaptive; admissibility descent dA/dt ≤ 0 says *stop when the admissible set stops shrinking* — easy queries pay max-effort tokens, hard queries get max only if the router knows they are hard.
- **Resolution:** confidence_head as the effort controller — non-think pass confidence routes the query up the ladder (low confidence → escalate); BRR's PID criterion (stop when dQ/dd ≤ 0 with bounded integral) as the descent rule. **Measure:** HLE 0.348 → 0.40+ at ≤ 2× tokens on hard queries; tokens/query per level on the registry.
- **Measured note (8/5, `hle_margin_probe.py`, qwen-1.5b on the HLE dev items):** **confidence alone is NOT a sufficient escalation signal — the model is confidently wrong.** 3/3 dev items scored 100% locked / 0 PLL unlocks (fluent, plausible, high-margin generations on items it cannot actually answer). A margin-only router would escalate NOTHING — false-negative risk. **Refinement: the effort router must be multi-signal — margin/lock PLUS a correctness-facing signal (self-consistency across n samples, or the deterministic verifier / BRR principle: verification beats confidence).** V4 validation: same multi-signal check with confidence_head, hardware-gated.

## 12. DSpark draft timing — jitter-gated speculation [hypothesis, Ortyx-derived]
- **V4 subsystem:** DSpark block 5 (7-token speculative window), markov_head (rank 256), confidence_head on mtp.2 only.
- **Corpus source:** Ortyx protocol ("Decomposition, Preservation, and Constraint-Preserving Reinterpretation of Speculative Computational Systems") — three transferable principles: (a) **jitter-tracking compression**: store deviation from the local average (peak-timing / amplitude jitter), not the full signal; (b) **structural consensus**: store the instructions, not the samples (SID-chip principle — structure over data); (c) **wave interference**: implicit association by frequency/phase similarity, no explicit lookup.
- **Edge case:** the block-5 window is fixed regardless of local token-stream stability. In low-jitter regions (code: high consensus, LiveCodeBench 91.6) block 5 is near-free; in high-jitter regions (novel reasoning) the markov draft is near-uniform — full draft cost, no acceptance. Domain-level telemetry (#8) is too coarse: jitter varies token-to-token *within* one query.
- **Resolution:** jitter-gated speculative windows — at inference, compute per-window **draft jitter** (variance of markov confidence over the window, or the top-1/top-2 logit margin — a free signal, no extra head needed); shrink the block to 1 when jitter exceeds a threshold (locally adaptive block size), and align window boundaries to low-jitter starts ("phase alignment": start drafting at consensus tokens, stop at jitter spikes). This upgrades #8 from domain-level to token-level adaptivity.
- **Measure:** DSpark accept-rate + tokens/query per jitter bucket (code vs HLE queries); expected: same acceptance at lower draft cost on mixed workloads, block stays 5 on pure code.
- **Feasibility note (MEASURED 8/5, `jitter_gate_feasibility.py`, qwen2.5-1.5b, 6 prompts / 1,189 tokens):** the free logit-margin signal is computable at inference (0 extra params), but the assumed domain separation is **FALSIFIED — inverted**: reasoning showed HIGHER margins (mean 7.91, jitter 0.091) than code (mean 6.04, jitter 0.152); %margin>1.0 was 93.4% vs 84.8%. Per-stream margin std (4.3–4.8) dwarfs the domain delta (1.9) → **jitter is token-stream-local, not domain-level** (confirms the #12 core claim, corrects its expected direction). Gate design implication: use a **tail-based gate** (block shrinks when the recent low-margin fraction, margin < ~1.0 ≈ 10–15% of tokens, exceeds a threshold) calibrated per-model on the actual confidence distribution — do NOT ship a domain-prior gate. V4 validation on hardware: same tail metric against confidence_head/markov variance.

## 13. Wave-order preservation — traveling waves + custodian for the CSA decay [hypothesis, mem8-derived]
- **V4 subsystem:** CSA pools (rate 4, 94% of KV) with importance-ranked decay scheduled (kv_decay_schedule: 2M→53%, 4M→25% retention).
- **Corpus source:** Mem|8 vs Wave-RNN whitepaper (`mem8/mem_8_vs_wrnn.md`): traveling waves preserve ORDER (prevent overwriting; Fourier-diagnosable diagonal bands); phase encodes temporal relationships; a **custodian** mechanism guards against runaway amplification/loop poisoning.
- **Edge case:** a pure importance-ranked retention schedule (catalog #4 resolution) can *re-order* the temporal stream — evicting old-but-important pools reorders what the indexer sees, and without a phase/order signal the decay is temporally blind. The Wave-RNN lesson: order preservation is a separate constraint from importance.
- **Resolution:** decay the *custodian* way — two signals per pool: importance (amplitude) AND age-phase (temporal order); eviction must preserve the subsequence order of retained pools (no reordering, only thinning) — the retention fraction schedule (53%/25%) applied as order-preserving thinning, not importance-sort-and-keep. Indexer-side: multiplicity-dampening (#2) already assumes stable pool identity — order-preserving thinning keeps that valid.
- **Measure:** at 2M ctx with 53% thinning: order-preserving vs importance-sorted retention → index diversity + MRCR-1M long-range recall; expect order-preserving to hold recall on sequential-dependency tasks (code, reasoning chains) at equal KV.
