### The Third "Hidden" Component

There is one more significant thing Godot relies on to build your final, one-click program: **Export Templates** .

Here's what they are and why they matter:

*   **What they are**: Godot needs a "template" to compile your project into a standalone executable . It's like a pre-made shell for the game's engine. On Windows, the folder path is something like `C:\Users\[your-username]\AppData\Roaming\Godot\templates\`  or in `%APPDATA%\Godot\` .
*   **How you get them**: These don't come with the main Godot download. You have to download them separately from the Godot website and place them in that specific folder .
*   **Why it matters**: Without the right export templates in that folder, you can't export your project to create that one, standalone executable you can double-click to launch the simulation .

### Beyond These Three Key Parts

Beyond the Godot engine and the export templates, Godot does use two other types of folders on your computer , but they exist as part of Godot's own internal system, not as things you need to create or manage separately.

1.  **The `user://` Folder**: This is where your final simulation will save things like user settings or high scores. You don't make this folder; your program creates it automatically when it needs to save data .
2.  **The Editor Data Folder**: This is a system folder where Godot keeps things like editor settings and the export templates we just talked about . It's separate from your project folder and you generally don't touch it directly.
